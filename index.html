01. Systems Over Screens

Don’t ask: "How should this screen look?" Ask: "What system produces this screen, and what governance produces that system?" True leverage is in designing the generative engine, not painting individual pixels.

02. Decision Ownership

A design system is a control system: its job is to make the wrong thing impossible to build. Value is measured in decisions permanently removed from the table, not components delivered in Figma.

03. Technical Fluency Without Ego

No throwaway redlines or speculative drawings. Bridging design directly into engineering contracts through DTCG tokens, Ant Design v5 composition, and deterministic DOM structures that QA test suites can automate flawlessly.

04. Delivery Economics (Time × Money × Risk × Scale)

Instead of "this button is inconsistent," think: "If we don't standardize this pattern, every squad will implement it differently, inflating frontend cycle time and creating exponential maintenance debt."

05. Self-Serve Federated Leverage

A sole architect across 14+ products cannot survive as a ticket-drawing factory. Turning design into an internal assembly kit where Product Owners compose pre-validated user stories autonomously without breaking the system.

06. Rejecting Design Theater & The Automation Asymmetry

Enterprise platforms have no time for decorative empathy posters or 6-week discovery slides. While engineering equips itself with automated compilers, it often treats design as a manual drawing sweatshop. I turn design into a high-velocity generative compiler—eliminating engineering and test costs before a single line of backend code is committed.

I operate where the cost of architectural decisions is high. In regulated enterprise software (InsurTech & FinTech), my focus is turning organizational ambiguity, conflicting stakeholder incentives, and complex business logic into scalable product platforms.
