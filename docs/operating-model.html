01.1

Months 1–3: The Dual-Track Reality

For my first three months, I was on two tracks at once — understanding existing company culture and supporting ongoing development, while simultaneously designing the system meant to replace it. The daily work inside existing cultural constraints was exactly where the real requirements lived.

01.2

Watching Technical Debt Form

I didn't inherit finished files on day one; I watched technical debt being created through months 1–4 while an external agency drew screens. They optimized for immediate stakeholder applause — free-canvas drawings with zero feasibility checks. I forced technical validation into reviews to protect engineering reality.

01.3

The Internal Problem: PO-Driven UI

Opening internal product files revealed Product Owners drawing screens by hand with no UX rules or benchmarks. Each screen was shaped by temporary weekly engineering capacity and raw client requests. To scale, UI had to be removed from immediate whim and placed under strict system governance.

02 My Architectural Thesis

A Design System is a Control System

Show an engineering team a mockup and they read it as a build order. My foundation's job isn't decoration — it's defining what engineers are permitted to see, making unscalable UI impossible to build at the source.

01

Control system over style guide

A design system's job is to remove decision friction from the table and make the wrong thing impossible to build. Value is measured in decisions removed, not components delivered.

02

Validation before aesthetics

A design without technical validation isn't a product — it is a picture. An approved picture that can't be built is technical debt with a stakeholder's signature on it.

03

One validation step ahead

Design earns its authority by running one validation step ahead of engineering — discovering, defining, and resolving edge cases before the backlog, never trailing it.

04

Evidence over authority

Inherited artwork is reclassified as an initial concept to be stress-tested in production code. A baseline you can stress-test beats a critique you can argue.

05

Predictability beats artistry at scale

At scale, predictability wins. One vocabulary, one pipeline, zero overrides — every screen resolves to the same primitives. Custom requests earn their place, or they wait their turn.

04 Stakeholder Audit & Governance

The Decision Trace Protocol

How I audit raw product owner requests through 4 mandatory steps to eliminate technical debt before engineering handoff.

The Raw Request
A product owner hands over a hand-drawn customer record mockup with inline form inputs, bespoke layout rules, and custom agency styling.
01
Diagnose Reference Class. This is an Object screen, not a dashboard. It must follow the Object floorplan template, rejecting blank canvas exploration.
02
Protect Read-First. Inline form fields create visual clutter and break read patterns. Form editing moves into a dedicated floating Drawer over a read-only record.
03
Reuse Before Custom. Every field maps strictly to an existing system primitive. Zero custom components; the one new request is pushed to the back of the backlog.
04
Property-Level Handoff. Delivered as an explicit contract of props, DTCG tokens, and AntD states — allowing engineering to implement directly without guessing.
The Governed Outcome
A buildable, zero-debt screen in the system's language — fully consistent with the other 86 screens, carrying no custom CSS overrides.
