# **The 7 Rules of Scalable Design for Legacy Insurance Platforms**

Insurance platforms are often built over decades, with legacy systems, scattered modules, and a mix of digital and offline touchpoints. Scaling these platforms without breaking functionality or overloading users requires a design mindset that balances **user experience, technical feasibility, and business strategy**. Drawing from experiences with Allianz Türkiye’s B2C apps and Agito’s insurtech solutions, here are the 7 rules of scalable design for legacy insurance platforms.

---

## **Rule 1: Prioritize Modular Architecture**
Legacy platforms are often monolithic, where a single change can ripple across the system.  
**Key principle:** Design modular interfaces and components that can evolve independently.  

- **Practical Insight:** At Allianz Türkiye, redesigning motor insurance pages revealed that separating policy management, claims, and onboarding into self-contained modules allowed future features to be integrated without breaking existing flows.  
- **Scalability Benefit:** Each module can be tested, upgraded, or replaced individually, minimizing downtime and technical debt.

---

## **Rule 2: Standardize Interaction Patterns**
Users of insurance platforms are diverse—from tech-savvy millennials to older clients—and expect consistency across channels.  

- **Core Approach:** Establish design standards for **navigation, form inputs, and microinteractions**.  
- **Example:** At Agito, creating a single design system for Health4All and GI4All allowed developers to reuse UI components across products, reducing inconsistencies in claims submission and policy management flows.  
- **Outcome:** Standardization accelerates onboarding, reduces errors, and ensures users can switch between modules seamlessly.

---

## **Rule 3: Design for Progressive Disclosure**
Legacy insurance platforms often overwhelm users with dense information—policy details, legal terms, add-ons, and claims history.  

- **Scalable Strategy:** Use progressive disclosure to show only what is essential at each step, revealing complexity on demand.  
- **Allianz Example:** In My Allianz, the redesign focused on **layered content hierarchy**, ensuring users first see key policy summaries before optional details, which reduced support calls and increased engagement.

---

## **Rule 4: Enable Cross-Channel Continuity**
Insurance users often start a process in one channel (web) and continue in another (mobile or in-person).  

- **Implementation Tip:** Centralize data and interactions, ensuring **session continuity and context preservation**.  
- **Agito Case Study:** Health4All’s cross-channel design allowed brokers to access the exact client progress, improving conversion rates while maintaining regulatory compliance.

---

## **Rule 5: Build for Flexibility, Not Perfection**
Legacy systems often limit flexibility due to technical constraints.  

- **Design Mindset:** Prioritize **configurable interfaces** over fully customized solutions. Allow business users to update products, rules, and content without full engineering cycles.  
- **Example:** At Allianz, enabling agents to update coverage options dynamically through the portal avoided long development cycles and improved time-to-market for new offerings.

---

## **Rule 6: Make Data Work for Design**
Scalable design isn’t just UI—it’s informed by analytics and user behavior.  

- **Methodology:** Continuously track user flows, error rates, and friction points. Use this data to iteratively refine interfaces.  
- **Practical Insight:** During the Allianz mobile banking usability study, analyzing user behavior in the money transfer flow revealed **high abandonment at multi-step verifications**, prompting redesigns that simplified confirmation steps without compromising security.  
- **Benefit:** Scalable design evolves alongside user needs, rather than stagnating with legacy assumptions.
